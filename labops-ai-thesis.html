01

The Problem

CAR-T and other autologous therapies are made one batch per patient. A single batch takes over 200 labor hours across manufacturing, QA, QC, and logistics, and labor is roughly 71% of batch production cost.1 Manufacturing and release alone frequently take two to four weeks, with QC testing and release typically two to three.2 Between 4% and 7% of patients never receive their CAR-T product because manufacturing failed.3

  • 200+ hrs

    Labor per CAR-T batch, across manufacturing, QA, QC, and logistics1

  • ~71%

    Share of batch production cost that is labor1

  • 4–7%

    Patients who never receive their CAR-T product due to manufacturing failure3

The manufacturing intelligence needed to run these processes safely and at scale does not exist as software. It lives in the heads of scientists and operators who are retiring, burning out, or moving on.

No institutional memory

Critical process knowledge is undocumented or siloed in individuals. When operators leave, the knowledge leaves with them.

Deviation blindness

Deviations are investigated days or weeks after the fact, often surfacing only at batch record review or release testing, when the patient's dose is already committed.

Regulatory drag

GMP documentation is manual, error-prone, and consumes operator time that should go to process improvement. Review-by-exception is the goal; paper makes it impossible.

Scale barrier

Processes validated at small scale break at commercial scale because no system captures what actually drives product quality.

02

Why Now

Paper is finally leaving the floor.

CGT CDMOs are moving to purpose-built MES platforms.4 That creates structured process data for the first time, which an intelligence layer needs.

FDA is writing the AI rulebook.

FDA's January 2025 draft guidance set out a risk-based credibility framework for AI used to support regulatory decisions on drugs and biologics,5 and CDER's 2026 guidance agenda includes AI/ML in pharmaceutical manufacturing.6

Unvalidated AI is now an enforcement issue.

In April 2026 FDA issued a warning letter to a drug manufacturer that had used AI agents to write its specifications, procedures, and production records, citing failures of Quality Unit oversight.78 The market needs AI that is validated, auditable, and keeps humans in the loop. That is the design premise of LabOps.AI.

The workforce can't scale fast enough.

Experienced CGT manufacturing talent is scarce, so the knowledge needs to be captured in systems rather than individuals.

What We're Building

LabOps.AI is an AI-native intelligence platform that captures, structures, and activates the process knowledge inside a biomanufacturing facility, connecting batch records, equipment data, deviations, and regulatory history into a unified operational brain. Every output is decision support: QA reviews and approves.
