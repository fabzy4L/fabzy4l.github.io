Nicotine and four brain genes, in humans and rats

qPCR · human dlPFC BA9 · rat frontal lobe · Front. Psychiatry 2023

Long-term nicotine exposure changes gene expression in the brain, but how sex and diet shape that change was largely unexplored. This study measured four genes in postmortem human prefrontal cortex from smokers and non-smokers, and in rats given nicotine on a regular or high-fat diet, to see whether the rat model moves the same way the human brain does.

01

The design

Humans: dorsolateral prefrontal cortex (BA9) from 12 smokers and 12 non-smokers, female and male. Rats: frontal lobe from female and male rats on a regular diet (RD) or a high-fat diet (HFD), given nicotine for 14 days by osmotic mini-pump; controls had a sham surgery.

The genes: CHRNA10, a nicotinic acetylcholine receptor subunit; CERKL, SMYD1 and FA2H, tied to sphingolipid metabolism, which matters for myelin. RNA from each sample was reverse transcribed and measured by qPCR.

02

Smokers and non-smokers

Each dot is one smoker, as fold change against the non-smoker average; the dashed line at 1 is the non-smoker level and the solid line is the smokers' geometric mean. The axis is logarithmic, so equal distances mean equal fold changes up or down.

Fold change vs non-smokers · human Ct normalized to GAPDH and RPL30 · hover a dot for its value · p-values as reported in the paper (SAS GLM)

Smokers had lower CERKL (p ≤ 0.0001), CHRNA10 (p = 0.0005) and SMYD1 (p = 0.0005), and higher FA2H (p = 0.0097). FA2H protein, stained in the same tissue, was also higher in smokers (p = 0.001).

03

Rats, by sex and diet

Rats on the regular diet, nicotine against sham controls, split by sex. Pooling the sexes hides the main result.

Fold change vs sham controls of the same sex · rat Ct normalized to GAPDH · one dot per animal · two males read about 0.001× for CHRNA10 and SMYD1, shown as recorded

CERKL depends on sex. Nicotine lowered it in female rats, matching the human direction, with no clear change in males on this diet; the sex difference was significant. On the high-fat diet the paper reports the same drop in females and a rise in males.

Diet changes FA2H: among nicotine-treated rats, the paper reports lower FA2H on the high-fat diet than on the regular one. CHRNA10 and SMYD1 moved up or down by sex without a significant sex effect.

04

What it shows, and its limits

In both species, nicotine exposure goes with changes in the same sphingolipid and nicotinic-receptor genes, which supports using the rat model for this question. In rats, sex and diet change the size and sometimes the direction of the effect.

The human tissue is postmortem and the exposure is a smoking history, not a controlled dose, so the human comparison is an association. Groups are small (12 people per group, six rats per sex and treatment on the regular diet), and the rats had two weeks of nicotine, not years. These are mRNA levels for four genes; the study does not show what they do to myelin or behaviour.
