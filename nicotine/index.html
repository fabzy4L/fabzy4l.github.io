Nicotine and four brain genes, analyzed per subject

qPCR · log10 linear models · sex × treatment · human dlPFC BA9 · rat frontal lobe

This is my analysis of the qPCR data from a study I co-authored on nicotine exposure in postmortem human prefrontal cortex and in rats. I kept every subject's own value, controls included, worked on a log scale and put sex in the model. That leaves two clear human changes, CERKL down and FA2H up, and a CERKL response in rats that depends on sex.

01

The design

Humans: dorsolateral prefrontal cortex (BA9) from 12 smokers and 12 non-smokers. Rats: frontal lobe from female and male rats on a regular or high-fat diet, given nicotine for 14 days by osmotic mini-pump, with sham surgery as the control.

The genes: CHRNA10, a nicotinic acetylcholine receptor subunit; CERKL, SMYD1 and FA2H, tied to sphingolipid metabolism, which matters for myelin. Human Ct values were normalized to GAPDH and RPL30, rat Ct values to GAPDH.

02

The analysis

Keep the control spread. Each control stays as its own measurement, so its variation counts against the effect. Setting controls to 1 would remove that variation and make the test too easy to pass.

Work on a log scale. Expression ratios are asymmetric: a 2-fold rise and a 2-fold drop sit at different distances from 1 until they are logged. I checked normality with Shapiro-Wilk before modeling. After log10, all four human genes pass in both groups.

Put sex in the model. A treatment × sex term tests whether the response differs between females and males, which splitting the groups and comparing p-values cannot do.

Count the tests. Sixteen rat comparisons will produce a few low p-values by chance, so the tables report results with that in mind.

CERKL is the gene to watch. On a regular diet it drops in females (0.58×) and not in males. On a high-fat diet it rises in males (1.57×) and not in females, with a treatment × sex interaction of p = 0.032. Females on a regular diet move the same way as human smokers.

These are leads, not settled effects. Across all 16 comparisons, the lowest corrected value is q = 0.32. Without the two low-reading males, CHRNA10 and SMYD1 show no treatment effect at all (p = 0.87 and 0.51), which is why I would check those wells before reading anything into them.

06

Limits

The groups are small, 4 to 6 animals per cell, so a result that isn't significant here is not evidence of no effect. The human tissue is postmortem and exposure is a smoking history, so that comparison is an association. Rats had two weeks of nicotine, not years. High-fat and regular samples may have run separately, so I do not compare expression across diets here. These are mRNA levels for four genes and say nothing direct about myelin or behaviour.

These numbers keep each control's variation, so they differ from the published figures, which plot fold change with controls set to 1. The paper reports its own statistics; everything on this page is my analysis, not a restatement of them.
